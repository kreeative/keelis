# Keewal Meere — conventions for contributors (human or agent)

Keewal Meere is a mobile-first fintech web app for **Africa** (React 19 + TypeScript + Vite). Products: Marchés (African equities + crypto), Chèque (spending + card), Épargne, and Change. UI copy is **French (fr-SN)**, with **en-NG** as the second locale; number/date formatting is localised via `src/lib/format.ts`.

## Money is multi-currency, and the currency decides its own punctuation
- **`src/lib/currency.ts` is the registry** — sixteen currencies: the two CFA francs, the major African units, plus EUR and USD as anchors. `Currency` is that union; `src/api/types.ts` re-exports it. It was `'CAD'`, a one-member union, and every African ambition in the roadmap was blocked behind it.
- **Not every currency has cents.** XOF, XAF, UGX and RWF are quoted in whole units; TND is quoted in *three* decimals (millimes). `formatMoney` asks the currency rather than forcing two, and the registry's `decimals` is pinned to `Intl`'s own answer by a test, so arithmetic and display cannot drift apart. Rounding happens **once, at the end** of a conversion (`roundTo`) — rounding to whole francs mid-calculation compounds across a two-leg cross.
- **Digits carry one punctuation app-wide: comma groups, point decimates** — `1,234,567.89`, in French as in English. This deliberately overrides French typography (thin-space groups, comma decimal) on the owner's instruction: it is what African fintech apps and every price feed already use, so a figure reads the same on screen as on the statement it is compared against. It lives in `joinParts`, so `formatMoney`, `formatNumber` and `formatPercent` cannot drift apart, and the keypad's decimal key shows a point. The *words* stay French. **The keypad's internal string still uses a comma** — `keypadReduce` and its tests work on that, and only the display converts — so do not "fix" it to match.
- **The locale matters for more than commas.** `fr-CA` renders XOF as "XOF"; `fr-SN` renders it "F CFA", which is what it is called where it is spent. That one substitution is most of what makes the app read as African rather than Canadian.
- **The space inside « F CFA » is widened, and that is a third deliberate override.** `Intl` puts U+202F between the two words — a narrow no-break space, correct French typography — and money in this app is tracked in at `--ls-numeric` (−0.035em), which applies to that space like any other character and very nearly cancels it. Measured on the home hero at 40.7px: the symbol with its narrow space is 31.23px and « FCFA » with no space at all is 31.14px. Nine hundredths of a pixel; the app was printing one word. `widenSymbol` in `lib/format.ts` swaps it for an ordinary no-break space, which survives the tracking with about two pixels left. Either the tracking is wrong or the space has to be wider, and the tracking is a measured decision about *digits* — a currency name is letters. It is fixed in the string rather than in CSS so that every renderer gets it: the hero, the account rows, a toast, an `aria-label`, a sentence built with `inlineMoney`. XOF is the only one of the sixteen whose symbol has an internal space.
- **A symbol is never inferred from the currency code.** `splitMoney` pulls it out of `Intl.formatToParts` — "F CFA", "₦", "GH₵", "€" — and reports which side the locale puts it on. Code that compared the code to `'CAD'` and assumed `'$'` is exactly what broke when the app moved.
- **The CFA peg is a treaty, not a quote.** XOF and XAF are fixed to the euro at **exactly 655.957**, so `lib/fx.ts` treats that pair as a constant: the rate does not move and the spread is the only variable. They are 1:1 with each other and still charged, because two central banks settle them.
- **The spread is stated, never buried in a worse rate.** `quote()` returns the mid rate, the applied rate, the tier and the fee *as money in the currency being sold*; `/convertir` shows all of them before the user commits. Tiers price legs, not sentiment: anchor 0.5 %, one African leg 1.2 %, African cross 1.8 %, pegged 1.0 %.
- **Quote a pair in the direction that reads.** XOF→EUR is 0,0015244 before the spread and 0,0015061 after — rounded for display both read "0,0015" and the margin looks like zero. Inverted (655,96 against 663,92 francs to the euro) the same spread is plainly visible. That is how a bureau de change writes it on the board.
- **The rates in `lib/fx.ts` are demonstration rates**, anchored to real orders of magnitude but not a feed. Every surface that shows one says so. The two pegged numbers are the exception and are exact.

## Brand
The marks live in `src/components/Wordmark.tsx`.

**The product was renamed from Keelis to Keewal Meere, and the wordmark artwork did not
survive it.** The old wordmark was a bezier path traced from the brand sheet — and that
path spelled *Keelis*. Artwork cannot be re-traced for a name it was never drawn for, so
the name is **typeset in Poppins as a stopgap** until new artwork exists. It is
deliberately not set in the brand face: EtherealDemo-ExtraBold has no commercial licence,
and the old wordmark only escaped that by shipping as outlines rather than as type.
**The monogram survives**, because the new name also starts with K.

- **The monogram is an image on purpose.** The sheet draws the K disc *embossed* — a raised
  letter, a lit rim, a soft shadow — and tracing it to a flat vector throws all of that
  away. `public/brand/mark-ink.png` and `mark-paper.png` are the sheet's own two versions,
  **tinted into the palette**: both exports were pure greyscale, which was invisible while
  the app was monochrome and read as a cold patch once it turned warm — the same failure the
  token guard now catches for `oklch(L 0 0)`. `scripts/tint-mark.mjs` maps each grey to the
  same *lightness* in the warm family, so the emboss is untouched (measured: a maximum
  luminance shift of 0.006 and identical alpha) and only the hue moves. The greyscale
  exports stay in `brand-src/`, which is not served, so a different warmth is one edit and a
  re-run rather than a re-export. That is a tint, not a trace — the rule below still holds;
  `--mark-disc` picks one by theme, so only the used one is fetched, and `tone="ink"` /
  `tone="paper"` pins one where the surface decides instead (the virtual card is always
  dark, so it always takes the paper disc). Do not replace it with a drawn circle.
- **The mark gets clear space, not paragraph spacing.** A logo set with the same air as a
  line of body text reads as crowded. Any screen that pairs the wordmark with a heading
  gives it `.mark-clear` — at least its own height before the next thing starts. Check the
  container's gap before reusing the class: it adds to it rather than replacing it.
- **The brand face is EtherealDemo-ExtraBold, a *demo* release not licensed for commercial
  use.** The outlines ship as artwork; never set the name as live text in it, and never add
  it as a webfont. Licensing it is the owner's decision.

## Commands
- `pnpm dev` — dev server · `pnpm build` — typecheck + build · `pnpm preview` — serve `dist/` on :4173
- `pnpm typecheck` · `pnpm test` (vitest) · `pnpm check:design` (static design-rule check) · `pnpm check:springs` (the easings still match the spring equation that generated them) · `pnpm check:marks` (the brand discs still match the artwork they are tinted from) · `pnpm check` (all five)
- **The repository is JavaScript and TypeScript, and nothing else.** `scripts/tint-mark.mjs` was Python — Pillow and numpy — for one 85-line script: a second toolchain and a second set of packages, in a repo that is otherwise entirely TS. It is Node with no dependencies now (`node:zlib` plus the PNG filtering, since the artwork is 8-bit RGBA and not interlaced), and it produces the committed discs byte-identically — verified pixel by pixel against the Python's output, 0 differing bytes of 102,400. Do not reach for a second language for a build step.
- `pnpm e2e` — Playwright screenshots + runtime audit (contrast, tap targets, overflow, shadows, anything left under the notch, any **sticky** control left under the floating nav, and anything **painted over**) against the preview server. Takes `--routes=`, `--widths=`, `--themes=` and `--no-shots` to narrow a run.
- `pnpm e2e:motion` — **measures the motion**, which is the one thing a screenshot cannot see at all: that the easings overshoot (a spring that never passes 1 is a bezier in a costume), that a sheet arrives *and leaves*, that the page behind it recedes without taking the floating nav off the bottom of the screen, that closing it hands the scroll back, and that a screen arrives from the direction you actually went.
- `pnpm e2e:flows` — **drives the money flows in a real browser**: buy a share, send through an operator, convert, add funds. Screenshots catch what a screen looks like on arrival; they cannot catch a button that stays disabled, a field that rejects what its own label asks for, or a back-end that refuses a phone number because it validates every handle as an email. All three of those were live defects this found.

## Non-negotiable design rules (enforced by `scripts/check-design.mjs` and `e2e/screenshots.mjs`)
- **All visual values come from `src/styles/tokens.css`.** Never write a colour literal, px font-size, shadow, blur or gradient in a component. Use `var(--…)`.
- **Surfaces are plain fills, and that is the newest decision in this file.** They were glass: translucent, heavily blurred, carrying `--glass-sheen` — a diagonal highlight across the top-left corner — and `--glass-rim`, three insets glowing along the edges. The owner pointed at that reflection, said it read as AI-generated, and asked for plain colours. A surface is now **a solid fill and a 1px hairline, and nothing else**: `.glass` / `.glass-strong` / `.glass-soft` + an `.elev-*` class, or the `Card` component which composes both. The class keeps its name because forty files use it and renaming buys nothing. `--glass-sheen` and `--glass-rim` are `none`; `--glass-blur` is gone, and every `backdrop-filter` in the library went with it — with nothing translucent left there is nothing behind a surface to blur, so it cost a composited layer per card and showed nothing. The `.glass::after` that carried the rim is deleted rather than emptied.
- **The hairline is now the whole separation.** With no blur, no sheen and no glowing rim, `--glass-edge` and `--glass-border` are all that stand between a card and what is behind it. They have to be a clear step from the fill in *both* themes — a border that reads at a glance in light and vanishes in dark is the failure mode here, and the contrast audit will not catch it, because contrast on a border is not text contrast.
- **Elevation is layered, never a single blur.** Use `--elev-1/2/3` (ambient contact + direct drop + deep lift) and `--elev-2-hover`. A hand-rolled `box-shadow` fails the design check.
- **Tactile physics.** Interactive surfaces lift with `var(--lift)` (`translateY(-2px) scale(1.01)`) and settle with `var(--press)`; both collapse to `none` under reduced motion. Animate transform and box-shadow only.
- **The page's ground is one flat colour.** `<AmbientGround />` (mounted once in `App`) paints `--surface` on a single fixed layer; `body` stays transparent on purpose — do not give it a background. It used to paint four overlapping radial fields, kept deliberately deep so a translucent pane over them read as translucent. Nothing refracts them now, so they only showed between the cards as a soft mottling — a gradient on a page asked to be plain.
- **The palette is one warm family: deep brown through to butter gold.** It was monochrome — every token `oklch(L 0 0)` — until the owner replaced that decision with the brown-and-gold reference. Sampling it, every surface, every piece of ink and the accent itself sit between **hue 62 and 97**: one family lit differently, not a set of colours. `scripts/check-design.mjs` enforces the band (55–105) and a chroma budget *by role* — the accent ramp (`--cta`, `--accent*`) may go to 0.14, everything else stays under 0.05, because the reference is emphatic that the surfaces are nearly neutral and only the accent is saturated. A surface that creeps up to the accent's chroma is how a restrained palette becomes a brown one. **Chroma 0 now fails too**, outside shadows and scrims: a pure grey among warm neutrals reads as a dead patch.
- **The gold is the signature, and it is a fill.** `--cta` is the butter gold in **both** themes (`#f0de9a` on dark, a deeper `#ddba56` on light so it holds against cream), with `--on-cta` the deep brown. That is the reference's own primary button, and the three places it appears are the three the eye should find first: the primary button, the active destination in the nav pill, and a selected chip or segment. `.canvas` deliberately does **not** remap `--cta` — it used to force it to paper, which was right when the primary button could only be the opposite of its ground, and which would now make the home screen the one place the app does not show its colour.
- **Links and focus rings are not the gold.** `--accent` is a bronze in light (`oklch(0.45 0.085 72)`) and the gold in dark, because pale gold as *text* on a cream page fails contrast however good it looks as a fill. Keep `--cta` for fills and `--accent` for anything that has to be read.
- **Two things still carry hue from outside the family, and only two.** **The first is direction**: `--pos` is green and `--neg` is red, because up and down is the one meaning people read by colour before they read anything — and neither can be warm without ceasing to mean what it means. Both are measured, and colour is never the *only* carrier: the explicit sign, the glyph and the weight still say it, so every state survives a greyscale screenshot. **The second is the asset marks** in `src/components/AssetIcon.tsx`, which keep each token's real colours — a logo is not decoration, it is how someone finds Bitcoin in a list without reading. The design guard exempts that file *by name*; putting a foreign hue anywhere else fails the check, and that is deliberate.
- **Direction is carried by hue *and* by sign.** Gains are `--pos`, losses `--neg` — but the explicit sign (`+` / `−`), the directional glyph and the weight carry it too, so nothing depends on colour alone. Errors resolve to `--neg` and carry their glyph — the glyph is what survives a greyscale screenshot, the colour is what makes a rejected field findable on a full form. Warnings stay `--ink-900` with their glyph. Keep `signed` on amounts that can go either way.
- **`--cta` is a fill only** (ink in light, paper in dark), reserved for primary buttons; text on it is `--on-cta`. Links, ghost buttons and focus rings use `--accent`; accent text on `--accent-soft` uses `--accent-text`.
- `--ink-300` is **not** for running text; use `--ink-400` for labels and tertiary text. The one exception is the empty amount's placeholder « 0 », which is 48px and therefore large text at a 3:1 floor rather than 4.5 — it holds 3,4:1, and the warm value had to be darkened to 0.60 to keep it there. Anything smaller in `--ink-300` fails, and the audit will say so.
- **The app defaults to the light theme, not to the system's preference.** The owner asked for it. « Système » is still one of the three choices and still follows `prefers-color-scheme` — but it is no longer what somebody gets without choosing, because a phone set to dark would otherwise open the app in a theme nobody picked for it, on the first screen most people ever see. This forced the storage to change: « système » used to be the key's *absence*, and absence now means light, so all three choices are written down and only an unrecognised value falls back. The pre-paint inline script in `index.html` resolves the same way, or a dark-set phone would flash dark before React corrected it.
- **The browser's own bar follows the *choice*, not the system.** `index.html`'s two `theme-color` metas are gated on `prefers-color-scheme`, which is right for the first paint and wrong the moment somebody uses the toggle: the media query still answers for the system. `applyTheme` therefore inserts an override meta ahead of them, **copying the colour out of the meta that already holds it** rather than carrying its own hex — a colour written in a second file is the one that goes stale. It is removed again when the choice returns to « système ». The stored choice is a bare string, not JSON: three e2e harnesses wrote `'"light"'`, matched nothing, and silently fell back to « système », which is why no walk had ever rendered the explicit `data-theme` path.
- **The chart line is smoothed with *monotone* interpolation** (`smoothPath`), never a plain spline. A Catmull-Rom or cardinal spline curves prettily and overshoots: between two points it swings past both and draws a peak higher than the highest price in the data. On a price chart that is a lie. Monotone (Fritsch–Carlson) clamps the tangents so the curve never leaves the interval its endpoints define — the corners round off, flat runs stay flat, and every high and low on screen actually happened. Covered by tests; do not swap it for something smoother.
- **The chart's area is texture that fades with depth.** A tinted fill under the curve would have to pick a hue — the line's green is a *meaning*, not a decoration, and tinting a whole region with it says the region means something too. So `Chart` fills the area with a lattice of 1px dots — it reads as filled without needing colour, and it is an SVG `<pattern>`, so it does not break the no-gradients rule. The lattice is masked by a vertical fade that reaches zero at 80% of the plot height, so the texture reads as something the line casts rather than a slab under it. The fade runs down the **plot box**, not down from the line — measured on the reference, ink at a given height is the same whether the line is 17px or 527px above it. It draws a dot on the last point, and on scrub a cursor line and a **ring** on the point being read — a ring, because a filled dot covers the very shape the cursor is asking about. **There is no tooltip.** The screen's own hero is the readout: `onHover` swaps the balance for the scrubbed value and the delta line for its timestamp, and a floating box repeating that sat directly on top of the number it was duplicating. One readout, where the eye already is; the scrubbed value still reaches screen readers through a visually-hidden `role="status"`. While scrubbing, the series is drawn twice — dimmed for the whole period, then again clipped to everything left of the cursor — so what you have scrubbed past stays lit and what you have not reached recedes. **There is no dashed baseline** — the opening-value line was removed on the owner's instruction: it ran horizontally through the middle of the plot and read as chart junk rather than as a reference. Do not reinstate it. The viewBox tracks the **measured** width via a callback ref — not a fixed 1000 — so the drawing space equals the painting space and dots stay round. A plain `useEffect` cannot do this: the chart renders a skeleton first, so there is no `<svg>` to observe yet.
- **A market chart carries a scale; a hero chart does not.** That is the reference's own distinction, and it is the single biggest thing this app was missing next to it: our curve was a line floating in an empty box, which says « it went up » and nothing else. `Chart` takes `axes` — round price levels labelled down the right, a handful of times along the foot — and it is **off by default**. **The hairlines are gone**: the scale used to draw one at every level and one along the foot, and the owner pointed at them on a dark asset page and asked for them to go. A label sitting at a height is a scale; a rule through the curve at that height was decoration, and four of them read as a ruled page. On Accueil and the savings page the figure the chart is about is printed directly above it and the scrub readout swaps into that same figure, so a scale there answers a question nobody asked; on an asset page the levels *are* the question. Same component, two jobs. The plot gives up the room rather than the labels sitting over the line: 56px on the right, 20px along the bottom, both measured. **And on the asset page « Acheter » / « Vendre » sit directly under the period control**, not in a sticky bar at the foot of the screen: the chart is what you read before you buy, so the buttons belong where the reading ends. From 1024px the pair keeps its seat in the aside beside the hero; it is rendered in both places and CSS shows one, the way the market row's venue is, so the DOM never carries two focusable pairs at once.
- **The levels are round, and the labels are exact.** `niceTicks` steps in 1, 2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten, so a scale reads 29,000 · 29,050 · 29,100 and not 29,013 · 29,067 · 29,121 — the whole reason a scale is worth drawing is that the levels are numbers a person already thinks in, so two glances at two periods are comparable. **The count bends to keep the numbers round, never the other way**: asking for four can give three. And `formatTick` never rounds a label, because a gridline marked « 93.7 M » sitting at 93,750,000 is a scale lying about where its own line is; the step says how many decimals the label needs, so « 93.75 M » is exact. It abbreviates only when the abbreviation is genuinely shorter — **compared, not guessed**: two heuristics in a row got it wrong, and a share at 29,190 F CFA drew a scale reading « 29.00 k », seven characters to replace six.
- **The scale carries no currency symbol, and neither does the O/H/B/C row.** Every tick on an axis is the same unit, and the hero directly above already says which — it is `moneyPair`'s rule applied to a column instead of a pair. The asset page's four figures (Ouverture, Haut, Bas, Clôture over the period on screen) are read off **the very series the chart draws**, never a second query, so the row cannot disagree with the curve above it; they keep full precision, because on a market screen the difference between 92,391,220 and 92,4 M is the part somebody came to read. The single letters are `<abbr>` with the French word, since a lone capital is nothing a screen reader can use.
- **The axis labels are HTML, not `<text>` in the SVG.** The svg carries `preserveAspectRatio="none"` so its viewBox can track the measured width, which means that between the first paint and the ResizeObserver's first callback the drawing is scaled — and scaled type is smeared type. As HTML they also take `--fs-label`, `tabular-nums` and `--ink-400` for nothing, and a screen reader skips them, because the whole series is already in the `<title>`.
- **The pro chart is `CandleChart`, and it is offered from 1024px only.** A candle packs four numbers into one mark; below a laptop there is neither the width to separate the bodies nor a pointer precise enough to read them, so the phone keeps the line — which is the right chart there. It is a **toggle, defaulted off**: the line is what most people want, and a candle chart shown to someone who did not ask for one is worse than no chart. Its candles are **bucketed from the very series the line draws**, never a second dataset — a pro view that disagreed with the figure above it would be worse than none. `candleCount()` sizes the buckets at ≥5 points each: slice a 96-point day into 48 candles and every bucket holds two points, so high === open, low === close, and every wick disappears (covered by a test — that regression is invisible in code and obvious on screen). Direction is carried by **fill as well as hue** — filled when it closed up, hollow when it closed down — so it survives a greyscale screenshot like every other state. **There is deliberately no volume histogram**: volume cannot be derived from a price series, and inventing one for an app aimed at real investors would look like information while being none. It needs a market data feed.
- **The market teaches, and every teaching button opens something.** `LearnSheet` holds the contextual explanations the roadmap asks for — what an introduction en bourse is, how to read a cours — in four or five sentences each, ending with what to *do*, including « rien pour l'instant » when that is the honest answer. Most people buying their first share on the BRVM have never bought one anywhere, and the words on the screen are what stops them, not the buttons. The « Comprendre une IPO » card used to carry a button with neither an `onClick` nor a `to`: it explained nothing and did nothing, which is worse than not offering.
- **`/crypto` is the market, and it lists both classes.** The page's hero is the whole book — `useLiveBook()`, the two investment accounts summed — because the list under it holds shares *and* coins; reading one account there would print a total that disagrees with the rows below it. `?classe=equity|crypto` opens the chip bar on one class, which is where the « Crypto » card on Accueil lands. The route stays `/crypto` even though « Actifs » is the equities account: renaming a path breaks links and buys nothing.
- **A market list is scanned by ticker.** `AssetIcon` + the symbol as the row's title, the full name in the subtitle, and `Delta` carrying `amount` so the move shows in money *and* percent — a percentage alone does not say whether it was worth 4 000 F CFA or 400 000.
- **That subtitle carries two facts on a phone and three from 768px.** It used to carry three always — `name · venue · quantity` — and « Dangote Cement · NGX · 9,000 DANGCEM » wants 274px in the 154 a 390px row can give it, so the tail was cut on every held row, and the tail is the quantity: the one fact on the line that belongs to the reader rather than to the market. Two things fixed it, both measured. The **venue drops below 768px** (`.venueWide`, CSS rather than a breakpoint hook, so there is no second render) — it is reference data, identical on every visit, and it is on the asset's own page. And the **quantity carries no unit at all**, because the row's title *is* the unit: « DANGCEM » sat at the head of the row and again at the end of its own subtitle, which was 85px of the width the line did not have. And the **name takes the ellipsis, not the tail**: `.value` never shrinks, so all the pressure lands on this line, and one `text-overflow` across the whole string cuts whatever is last — which was the quantity. The name is a flex item that truncates and the facts are `flex-shrink: 0`, so « Dangote Cement · 9,000 » spends its missing ten pixels on « Dangote Cem… · 9,000 » instead. Every quantity and venue now renders in full at 390px; only two names clip, by 3 and 6px. **The first separator is a no-break space** — `.assetSub` is a flex container, an ordinary leading space is collapsed as the start of its item's line box, and the rows read « Sonatel· 160 ».
- **More filters than fit on a phone → `ChipBar`, not `SegmentedControl`.** The segmented control divides a fixed width between its segments, so it caps out at three or four; the chip bar is content-sized and scrolls past the gutter, and that overflowing chip is the affordance saying there is more.
- Assets are drawn with `AssetIcon`, never a monogram `Avatar`, wherever the symbol is known. `AvatarStack` takes `assets` for the same reason.
- **Gradients are back, and they live only in `tokens.css`.** The owner asked for plain colours once and for «　des dégradés　» later; the later instruction is the one in force. What survives from the old rule is the half that was doing the work: the failure mode was never «　a gradient exists　», it was a look returning **one component at a time**, each file individually defensible and the sum of them not. So `scripts/check-design.mjs` no longer forbids a gradient — it forbids one written anywhere *but* the token layer, where it is a decision made once with its reasoning beside it and undone in one edit.
- **They are value-falls, not sheens, and that distinction is the whole design.** What read as generated was `--glass-sheen`: a *diagonal* highlight across a corner, implying a lamp the screen does not have, on a surface that is not an object. These run top to bottom, the direction light falls, and only on things that **are** objects — a button you press (`--grad-cta`), a key on a pad (`--grad-key`), the gold capsule in the nav, a selected chip. A panel of text stays flat. The payment card is the one diagonal (`--grad-card`), because a real card is laminate held at an angle and is the only surface here that is a physical thing in a hand. Each gradient is **centred on the flat token it replaces** — `--grad-cta`'s midpoint is `--cta` to three decimals — so it adds material without restating the palette, and a test pins that. The travel is 0.06 of lightness, about a sixth of a decorative gradient; `--grad-key` is 0.015, because twelve keys repeating a visible gradient is a texture rather than twelve of the same object. `--grad-canvas` **starts at exactly `--canvas-bg`** and only darkens downward, since the top of the page is what `index.html`'s `theme-color` is measured from — and `.canvas::before`, which carries the colour 100vh up for an iOS overscroll, stays flat so the two join with no seam.
- **A gradient is two backgrounds, so contrast is measured at both ends.** `--on-cta` was checked once against the flat `--cta`; the moment that fill became a value-fall, the label at the *bottom* of the button sits on the darkest stop, and nothing was looking at it. `tokens.test.ts` now reads every stop of every gradient. **And introducing them silently blinded the runtime contrast audit**: a gradient-filled element reports `background-color: rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)`, so `effectiveBg` walked straight past the primary button to the canvas behind it and called «　Envoyer　» 1.03:1 — a number, confidently wrong, about the control the app most needs to get right. It reads gradient stops now and reports the **worst** one, because an audit reports the floor; the stops go through a canvas rather than a regex, since Chromium returns a computed colour in the space it was authored in and every token here is `oklch()` — the third time that particular trap has been paid for in this repo. The design check had the same hole from the other side: it used `line.match`, one colour per line, so `--grad-card`'s three stops were checked as one and a foreign hue could ride in on stop two. It reads them all now, and that is falsified by a test rather than assumed.
- No emoji. **No `backdrop-filter`** — it fails the design check outright, with no allowlist, and it does not come back with the gradients: it was removed because the surfaces are opaque, so there is nothing behind a card to blur. A value-fall on a fill costs a paint, not a composited layer.
- Text ≥ 12px (`--fs-label`). Tap targets ≥ 44px (`--tap`).

- **The address form exists once**, as `AddressFields` in `features/shared` — the six controls, « clear the région and the postal code when the country changes », and which countries have a postal code at all. Onboarding wrote it first and this screen needed the same thing; two copies is how a Sénégalais address becomes valid on one screen and refused on the other. `places.ts` moved to `src/lib/` with it, because a list of countries is data, not an onboarding concern.
- **A form seeded from a query must stop seeding the moment somebody types.** This one got it wrong in both directions, and each way was invisible. Re-seeding whenever the query resolved meant a background refetch landed mid-sentence and put the stored values back over what had been entered — *silently*, so the only symptom was a Save that appeared to do nothing. Seeding strictly once then meant seeding from the **session**, which is a snapshot written at sign-in and can predate the address entirely: the form opened blank and Save never enabled. The condition is neither « once » nor « whenever » but « has this person touched it », which is a ref, not a dependency. And the form reads `profile.me()`, not `useSession()`: the session is for auth, the server's copy is the one that is true.
- **A save writes to both copies of the user.** `refreshUser()` updates the session the avatar and the profile header render from; `setQueryData(QK.me, …)` updates the cache the form itself seeds from. With only the first, navigating away and back showed the old name beside a header showing the new one.
- **Choosing among options is a `ChoiceList`**, not a `<select>`: the kit builds its list selector out of universal rows with a radio trailing, so each option can carry the facts that decide the choice (fee, delay, last payment). It is a real radiogroup — one tab stop, arrows move and select, disabled options are skipped — and takes a `footer` for the escape hatch. Keep `SelectField` for flat lists of plain labels.
- **`ListRow stack`** is the kit's mobile row: below 768px the value drops under the text instead of competing with it for width, while the leading mark and the trailing control span both lines. Use it when a row carries both a long label and a value.
- **`StatGrid`** is the kit's Stats block, with both of its shapes: label-left / value-right rows under 768px, a label-above-value grid beyond it.
- **Unread markers** use `StatusDot`: 8px with a 2px ring of `--surface`, and `corner` anchors it to the top-right of the *glyph's* wrapper, never the 44px control around it.
- **Icons** are a 20px box (`--icon`, the reference kit's grid size) holding a ~16px glyph, lucide outlines at 1.5px stroke. `Icon` defaults to 20; pass `size` only where a screen needs otherwise. The `ICONS` map ends with a semantic group taken from the kit — `deposit`, `withdraw`, `transfer`, `recurring`, `cheque`, `wire`, `crypto`, `invest`, `split`, `face-id`, `warning` and friends. Prefer the semantic name over a generic arrow: it says what the action means and survives a change of glyph.

## Photography
- **The app is image-led, and the photographs are committed.** `public/photos/README.md` is the brief: WebP and JPEG across two widths, ~120 KB at 800 and ~320 KB at 1600, and a **dark subject under a single warm light** for anything type sits near. The images **must** be in the repository, because the site builds from it — an ignored photograph is one the deployed app never has. That reverses an earlier rule here, and the reversal is the lesson: `.gitignore` over `public/photos/` looked like caution and actually guaranteed the feature could not work. **What must never ship is a placeholder**, and a path cannot tell one from a photograph, because a stand-in and the real thing share filenames by design — that is what makes dropping the real ones in a no-code change. So the guard is in the bytes: `scripts/photo-placeholder.mjs` stamps every frame with a PNG `tEXt` marker and a test fails on any image carrying it. A marker survives a rename; a path does not. The licence is still the owner's to hold, and the `credit` is printed on `/entreprise` — a photograph on screen with nobody named is that page's own promise broken in another medium.
- **There is no AVIF, and that is a measurement rather than a preference.** `scripts/photo-import.mjs` encodes through the Chromium this repo already has — no image library, because the repository is JavaScript and that rule already paid for itself once with `tint-mark.mjs`. Probed before it was written: `canvas.toDataURL('image/avif')` neither throws nor returns null, it **silently hands back a PNG**. Behind `<source type="image/avif">` that serves PNG bytes, several times the weight, to exactly the browsers modern enough to be offered the format, with nothing anywhere reporting a fault. The importer now refuses any encode whose returned type is not the one it asked for. Adding AVIF back means adding a real encoder, not re-adding the line.
- **The importer never upscales, and the slot records what exists.** A 1000px source asked to fill a 1600px slot is the same pixels in more bytes, and a `srcset` naming a width no file has sends the browser after a 404 at the first paint of the front door. `widths` is per slot, a test checks every file a live slot promises is actually there, and the importer prints the line to paste.
- **The crop belongs to the picture, not to the component.** `focus` is a per-slot `object-position`, because one default served the welcome hero and **beheaded the mask on `/entreprise`**: the weighting that keeps a tall subject clear of the sheet cuts the face off a portrait dropped into a landscape band. The band is also `clamp(300px, 72vw, 400px)` rather than 200px — a portrait source in a short landscape box shows the top of a head and nothing else — and it carries **no scrim**, because a wash exists so type can sit on a picture, nothing sits on that one, and darkening a frame shot against a pale ground only turns the ground grey.
- **`src/lib/photos.ts` is the manifest, and the app reads it rather than the folder.** A slot with `present: false` renders nothing and its screen falls back to the type-only layout that shipped before photography existed — so a missing file is a quieter design, never a broken one, and the app is shippable both before and after the pictures arrive. The manifest is also the commissioning brief: every slot carries an `intent` a photographer could work from, and a test refuses an intent too short to be one. Switching a slot on **requires a credit and a describing sentence** — another test — because « fill those in later » is exactly what ships without them, and « image » is what a screen reader says when nobody wrote one.
- **`Photo` is a `<picture>`, and that is a performance decision before it is a markup one.** Most of the people this app is for are on a phone on mobile data; a 1600px JPEG served to a 390px screen is the difference between a screen that arrives on a taxi connection and one that does not — the same connection `RouteBoundary` exists for. WebP first, JPEG behind it, `sizes` giving the browser the real measure before it picks a file, `width`/`height` always emitted so the box is reserved before the bytes land, and `priority` (eager + `fetchPriority: high`) only on the one image a screen is *about*. There is deliberately **no placeholder photograph**: inventing a stand-in means shipping somebody else's picture.
- **Text on a photograph is the one text in this app that no check can measure, and it is still decidable.** Every audit here reads computed styles, and a picture has no colour to read — so this is the gradient hole one level worse: there the colour existed and the walk stepped past it, here there is genuinely nothing to read, and an audit that reports a ratio is inventing one. `Photo`'s scrim makes it computable: the audit interpolates the scrim's alpha at the text's **own height** in the frame and composites it over **white**, the brightest photograph that could ever be dropped into that slot. Text that clears the threshold against that clears it against anything the owner licenses — a real guarantee, checkable before the photographs exist. With no scrim it reports `contrast-unmeasurable` rather than a number.
- **`scripts/photo-placeholder.mjs` exists so the illustrated layout is rendered rather than trusted.** It writes obviously-fake stand-ins — a warm value-fall with a lit ellipse and the word PLACEHOLDER struck across it, each stamped with the `KEEWAL-PLACEHOLDER` marker a test looks for — under the real file names, as PNG bytes, since a browser decodes by content rather than extension and that is the same code path the real files take. No dependency and no second language. The reason it exists is this repository's own record: the notch, the scrollbars and the dark theme were each code that looked right and had **never been rendered under the condition it was written for**. A seam nobody has run is a seam nobody has tested.
- **The photograph has no edge where it begins, and that was the biggest thing missing.** The reference dissolves: cream at the status bar, fading through the sky of the picture, with its title sitting over where the ferns start. Ours drew a **hard horizontal rule straight across the screen** at exactly the point the eye arrives. `--scrim-dissolve` pours the page's own colour down over the top of the frame — the opposite operation from a scrim, at the other end, so it rides on `::before` and a frame may carry both. It is `--surface` *exactly*, written out per theme rather than `var(--surface)`, because a ramp needs both ends and CSS cannot take an alpha of a custom property without `color-mix` greying the midpoint. 22% deep: shorter and the seam still shows, longer and it eats the subject.
- **Overlapping the title onto the frame was tried, measured and reverted.** It is what the reference does, and it works there because its photograph has a pale sky; ours is a dark corridor, so pouring cream over it put the whole lede on the mask's cowries — illegible in the screenshot. **The audit called it clean**, which is the finding: `effectiveBg` walks the DOM, so it sees a frame the text sits *inside* and is blind to one the text merely sits *over*. A hero behind a type block by z-index is a **sibling**, and the walk went straight past it to the page colour and reported a perfect ratio against a background no reader ever sees. Geometry answers what ancestry cannot — `document.elementsFromPoint` at the middle of the line — and it is falsified: with the overlap in place it flags both the title and the lede, and clean once reverted.
- **The onboarding steps stopped stranding their own form.** `.body { flex: 1 }` pinned the action to the thumb, which is right, and left the field marooned at the top: on `/inscription/courriel` one short input, then **four hundred measured pixels of nothing**, then «　Continuer　» — on all nine steps, because they share `StepShell`. `justify-content: flex-end` is the whole fix: the question stays at the top, the form and its action sit together where the hand is, and a step whose body is already tall (the NIP keypad, the document upload) is unaffected.
- **A field may carry its leading mark on a disc** (`chip` on `Field`), from the reference's own fields: an icon alone in a pale row reads as a stray glyph, and the same icon on a circle reads as a fitting — which says « this line is for an address » before the placeholder is read. Pale, not filled: it labels the field and must not compete with what gets typed into it.
- **The front door is the sign-in.** The owner put the reference beside ours and asked for the same, and the difference was not the anatomy — that was already taken — but what the sheet *carries*: theirs holds the login, ours held three buttons, one of which led to a screen that held the login. Signing in is the front door for the person the app is for, and a button that leads to a door is a hallway. `/bienvenue` now carries the address and the code as two **pill fields** (`pill` on `Field`: `--r-pill`, and `--control-lg` tall, because a 44px control has to sit *inside* one with air on both sides), each with a **bare icon** at its head — the disc under it was tried and the owner read it as a shadow — the code's field carrying **« Recevoir » as a pill at the end of its own line** — the reference's « I forgot », which is the same situation: a field that cannot be filled until that pill is pressed. It reads « Renvoyer » after the first send, and only for the address it was sent to. The in-field pill is ink, the reference's own colour for it; **the call to action stays gold**, because the brand outranks the kit and two gold fills on one sheet would say nothing about which to press. Enter does the next thing, whichever it is. A right code with no account behind it goes to `/inscription`, which resumes from the state the server just opened — nobody is told « wrong address » for an address that is simply new. « Créer un compte » is a text action under the button, and the demo is a line in the footer. `/inscription/courriel` stays as the wizard's own first step, since the wizard still needs one.
- **The photograph is the welcome screen's underlay, not a band in it.** The owner asked for the image expanded under the sign-in, and that is the reference's composition too: the picture fills the frame from the top edge, the label row, the mark and the sentence sit on its dissolved top third, the mask emerges under the sentence, and the sheet rides over its foot. Three measurements decided the shape. **The frame stops at 80% of the screen** (`inset: 0 0 20%`): the sheet covers everything below that, and a shorter frame is a *smaller* picture under `cover`, which is what lifts the mask's face — rows 31–51% of the file — into the band between the sentence and the fields; at full height it sat under the code field. **The dissolve is deep** (`--scrim-underlay`, `dissolve="deep"` on `Photo`): opaque to 31% and gone by 42%, because the type sits *on* the frame now and the contrast audit composites the ramp at the text's own height over black and over white and takes the worse — so any alpha under the sentence is a ratio against a picture nobody has chosen. Falsified: thinned to 12%, the audit flags « Bienvenue » at 4.20:1 against the computed floor. **And the sign-in stays on the first screen** — the document is exactly 844px on an 844px phone, which the flows suite asserts, because it is the one thing a login must be and the one thing a fullPage screenshot cannot see. Before this the portrait file set the frame's own height and the form began 647px down; the mark is `md` and the title is one line for the same reason. The image is free for anyone to use, on the owner's word, and the credit says so; the photographer is still unnamed.
- **Text over a photograph is measured through its dissolve, and an opaque sheet ends the question.** `overPhoto` used to declare any text stacked over a frame unmeasurable; it now hands the frame to `scrimFloor`, which reads `::after` (the scrim) and `::before` (the dissolve) and composites them in paint order. And the walk through `elementsFromPoint` stops at the first *ancestor* that paints an opaque fill — with the frame under the whole screen, every line on the sheet was otherwise « on a photograph » that nobody can see through the sheet.
- **The one heading at the body weight.** The reference sets its welcome line thin, and a 600 sentence over a photograph is a headline where a greeting is wanted. `.title` on this screen takes `--fw-body` with `--ls-body`, since negative heading tracking is what keeps a demi sentence from spreading and reads pinched on a light one. It is the one heading in the app that is not about anything — it is hello — and that is the whole of why it is allowed.
- **The welcome screen's anatomy comes from the owner's reference**: one word across the top with the theme control (the row opened with « Keewal Meere » as a label and the owner asked for it to go — the brand on this screen is the K, and a name typed beside the mark was a second wordmark nobody drew; the toggle keeps its 44px hit area and draws a 32px disc inside it, because the full disc was too big for a preference in a corner), the monogram and the sentence, a **full-bleed** hero, and a rounded sheet riding up over the frame carrying the actions. The hero is unrounded on purpose — it was inset in the gutter at `--r-panel` with the sheet's `--r-sheet` directly under it, two radii arguing at the one seam the eye is already on. A hero is the element that bleeds; the only rounding in the composition is the sheet riding over it.

## Architecture
- `src/api/types.ts` — the `KeewalApi` contract. `src/api/mock/` implements it (latency, events, price ticks, optimistic settlement). Screens import `api` from `@/api` only.
- `src/store/` — `useQuery` (cache; data never drops to undefined during refetch), `useMutation`, `QK` query keys, `useSession` (auth + PIN lock), `useSettings` (theme / locale / hidden balances), `useToast`, `useMarket` / `useAsset` (live prices), `useOnline`.
- `src/components/` — the component library. Import from `@/components`. Do not create parallel primitives; extend these.
- `src/features/<area>/` — screens. Routes live in `src/shell/routes.tsx` (French paths).
- **Nothing sticky may sit under the floating nav, and `pnpm e2e` now checks it.** The rule below — anything at the bottom of a screen clears the pill with `calc(var(--navbar-height) + var(--navbar-gap) * 2 + var(--safe-bottom) + …)` — was written down and never verified, and it fails *silently*: the covered button is present, focusable and reachable by keyboard, so the keyboard walk passes and a screenshot looks fine apart from one control being half a pill short. **Only sticky and fixed elements count.** An ordinary control that happens to be at that height scrolls out from under the pill the moment anybody touches the screen; the first version of the check ignored that and reported 54 « violations », almost all of them accordion rows on `/profil/aide`, with the two real ones buried in the middle. A check that cries wolf on a scrollable page is worse than no check.
- **A control can be present, focusable and paint nothing, and `pnpm e2e` now checks that too.** It is the one failure mode none of the other checks can see, because what it looks like is *nothing*: the contrast audit has no pixels to measure, the tap-target audit measures a button that is there, the keyboard walk finds it visible and ringed — it is — and the screenshot shows an empty band, which is what an empty band looks like. It took Accueil shipping a heading and two buttons that had never once been on screen. `paintedOverViolations` forces `pointer-events` on for every element **and every pseudo-element**, so `elementFromPoint` reports what is really on top (a `::before` answers as its owner), and asks five points across each control and heading; it flags only a target where every point lands on an unrelated element that paints an opaque fill. **A `fixed` or `sticky` ancestor is excluded** — the nav pill sits over the foot of every page and the control under it scrolls clear the moment anybody touches the screen, which is the under-nav check's business and is the same lesson it learned. And alpha is *parsed*, not matched against `rgba(…)`: Chromium reports a computed `background-color` in the space it was authored in, so every token here comes back `oklch(…)` and an `rgba`-shaped regex reads every surface in the app as transparent. The first version did exactly that and found nothing at all.
- **Navigation floats at every width.** The desktop rail is the mobile pill stood on its end — detached from all four edges, `--glass-bg-strong`, `--r-pill` and `--elev-2`. Detached is the point: a rail flattened against the viewport edge stops reading as an object sitting *over* the page, and the shadow is what says so now that there is no rim. **Mobile navigation is a floating pill**, not a bar welded to the edge: centred, `--elev-2`, with the active destination in a filled capsule and icons only (each link carries its name as an `aria-label`). Anything sticky at the bottom of a screen must clear it — `calc(var(--navbar-height) + var(--navbar-gap) * 2 + var(--safe-bottom) + …)`.
- **Accueil is a canvas with a sheet riding over it, and the canvas follows the theme.** It used to be dark in both themes, the way the virtual card still is; the owner asked for it white on the light theme. The `--canvas-*` set therefore has two versions — in light they resolve to the page's own values, so the token island inside `.canvas` becomes a no-op and nothing has to be restyled; in dark they carry the dark island exactly as before. It is **not** `--card-surface`: the virtual card is still always dark. `index.html`'s `theme-color` is the canvas's own measured colour (#fcfcfc / #0f0f0f), because the top of the page is the canvas and a `theme-color` taken from the page background is what put a pale seam above the app. The canvas holds the balance, the curve, and — at its foot, under the curve — the two actions people actually take: the balance and its shape are what the screen is for, and the actions are what you do after reading them. The canvas carries its own colour up past the viewport (`.canvas::before`) so an iOS rubber-band overscroll reveals more black instead of the light ambient ground. **The sheet is `--sheet-ground`, not `--surface`** — grey, a step below the white glass cards riding on it. When the sheet was `--surface` too, the cards had nothing but their own shadow to be seen by; the separation is the material, not the fill, and the token is a step below the card in *both* themes rather than flipping. The canvas the sheet carries everything else and rides up over the canvas's rounded bottom. It is always dark, in both themes, the way the payment card is — and it does not restyle its children: it **redefines the tokens they read** (`--ink-900`, `--surface`, `--pos`, `--glass-bg`…) from the `--canvas-*` set, so a chart, a segmented control and a button behave inside it exactly as they would on a dark theme. Adding a component there costs nothing; adding a hand-written dark style costs the next person an hour.
- **A hero figure never wraps — it shrinks to fit.** "10,792,539 XOF" broke across two lines mid-number and mid-symbol, which is the one thing a hero amount cannot do. `AmountDisplay` counts the characters and passes a ceiling in container units (`--amount-fit`): a tabular Poppins digit is ~0.55em wide, so N characters fit at `(100 / (N × 0.55)) cqi`, and the CSS takes `min()` of that and the display size. Short amounts are therefore untouched at full size, long ones step down exactly as far as they need to, and it adapts to the *container* rather than the viewport — no measuring loop, no layout thrash. Measured: 21,838,776 F CFA renders on one line at 40.7px on a 390px screen and 32.7px on a 320px one.
- **A figure is one size throughout — and it carries no `,00`.** `formatMoney` drops the cents on a whole number by default (`alwaysCents` opts back in), because `,00` carries no information; a dozen call sites were already opting out of it one at a time before it became the default. The decision is made on the value *rendered*, not the one passed in, or a tiny negative clamped to zero would print `0,00 $` while a true zero printed `0 $`. What remains — real cents, and the currency symbol — is set at the same size, weight and ink as the dollars. `AmountDisplay` used to halve the cents and grey them; the owner asked for one size and there is no `.cents` split any more.
- **One curve per screen, and it belongs to that screen.** Accueil's chart is the *whole* wealth; the Crypto page draws its own book. The account cards and the `Actifs` row carry **no sparkline** — direction there is a signed figure in `--pos` / `--neg`, money *and* percent, because a percentage alone does not say whether the move was worth 4 000 F CFA or 400 000. A second small curve on a tile competes with the one above it and says less.
- **The book's curve is computed, not generated** (`api.crypto.portfolioHistory`): each holding's real price series times the quantity actually held, summed point for point. A portfolio chart drawn from its own random walk would disagree with the assets listed under it, and the first person to notice would be right.
- **Accueil carries four account rows: Chèque, Épargne, Actifs, Crypto.** « Actifs » held African equities *and* crypto in one account until the owner asked for crypto to stand on its own — which is how any institution offering both keeps them, since one is a regulated security on a local exchange and the other is not. `AccountKind` has four members; `investing` is the equities account and `crypto` means what it says. The two balances come from the same holdings by the same arithmetic (`bookValue(class)`), so they always add up to what the single account used to show, and a trade lands on the account its asset belongs to — a Sonatel purchase must never appear under Crypto.
- **They are rows, not tiles: name on the left, money on the right, and nothing else.** A stacked label-balance-delta tile is three lines to say what a statement says in one, and four of them filled the screen. The name takes the ellipsis and the figures never shrink — the money is the reason for the row. The Actifs row used to carry an `AvatarStack` of the asset marks and the owner asked for it to go: on a row whose job is « this account, this much », a strip of logos answers neither, and it was squeezing the account's own name at 320px. What is held belongs on the page the row leads to — and dropping it also removed the `useHoldings()` call Accueil made on every visit for something nothing else rendered.
- **Cash accounts carry no delta at all.** Chèque and Épargne used to print their twenty-four-hour movement, and it was never a return: a chequing balance moves because you spent, and calling that a loss in red is a small lie told four times a day. The **investment accounts keep direction, as a signed percentage and nothing else** — the money figure that used to sit beside it was the widest thing on the row and said the same thing in another unit. The right column's second line is a rate for Épargne, a direction for the two books, and nothing for Chèque.
- **The savings rate is « 4,00 % par an », never « APY ».** That acronym is American, means nothing to somebody reading French in Dakar, and is the wrong word besides: it promises a *compounded* yield, while what this pays is a simple daily rate on the balance credited monthly. `formatRate` lives in `lib/format.ts` rather than in `features/savings/`, because the account row was writing « 4.0 % » with `formatPercent` while the savings page wrote « 4,00 % » — the same rate, two ways, on two screens a tap apart. Its no-break space is a real one: the constant behind the old helper was *named* `NBSP` and held U+0020, so « 53 % » could break across two lines between the figure and its sign.
- **One currency, one symbol.** Two amounts in the same currency written as a pair go through `moneyPair`, which states the unit **once**, on the side the locale puts it — « 1,850,000 / 3,000,000 F CFA » in fr-SN, « ₦1,850,000 / 3,000,000 » in en-NG. Saying the same word twice about the same money is not free: on the savings goal card the second « F CFA » was 32 of the 210px the figures took, and the figures do not shrink, so it came out of the goal’s *name* — the one thing on that card the person wrote themselves. Both sides go through the ordinary formatters (`formatMoney` for the side that keeps the symbol, `splitMoney` for the bare digits), so a pair cannot punctuate itself differently from every other figure on screen, and the `aria-label` still spells out « … sur … », because two bare numbers and a unit is not a sentence.
- **Ask the card’s width, not the viewport’s.** The goal card stacks its name above its figures below 320px *of card*, and that is a `@container` query, not a media query. It was `min-width: 768px`, and it was wrong at both ends: the goals panel sits in a 340px aside from 1120px up, whose card line is 306px — **narrower than the 324px a 390px phone gives it**. So the widest screen got the layout meant for the widest card, and « Fonds d’urgence » rendered « Fonds d’… » on a 1440px display while fitting whole on a phone. Any row whose width is set by a column rather than by the screen has the same trap.
- **A balance is abbreviated only when it genuinely will not fit — and millions do fit.** `formatMoney(v, { compact: true })` measures the *string*, not the value. `COMPACT_MAX_CHARS` is **18**, measured on the account row at 320px: the row is 254px, the widest name 66 and the gap 16, so the amount has 172px and runs about 8.75px a character — nineteen fit, twenty do not, and eighteen leaves the name 90px instead of shaving the last six pixels. That covers every balance below a billion francs, so « 13,545,512 F CFA » prints in full. It was 15, from a first estimate that guessed « about 170px » and the per-character width rather than measuring either, and it abbreviated figures with thirty pixels of room to spare. When a figure really is too long the suffixes are the locale's own (k / M / Md in French, K / M / B in English) rather than a table this app would maintain for sixteen currencies, and it is **truncated, never rounded** — 19,999,999,999 rounds half-up to « 20 Md », money the account does not hold. `Money`'s `aria-label` is always the whole figure: an abbreviation is a way of fitting a number into a box, and a screen reader has no box.

- **The demonstration portfolio holds African shares, and they are the larger half of it.** It used to be four coins and none of the seven listed equities, in an app whose roadmap opens « l'app porte principalement sur l'achat d'actions africaines » — a product arguing against itself on its own home screen. Sonatel is the largest single position on purpose. The buys that produced the book are in the transaction seed too, so the activity list shows the thing the app is for actually happening.
- **A purchase is `asset_buy`, not `crypto_buy`.** Buying a share of Sonatel and buying a bitcoin are the same movement from the account's point of view, and the app lists both under « Actifs »; the old names described half the product.
- **Every section sits on a surface.** Balance, chart and period control are one statement, so they share one `Card`; a list goes in a card; interest figures go in a card. Content floating loose on the page ground is the exception, not the default — the ground is the ground, not a place to set text. Section headers stay *outside* the card they introduce.
- Layout: wrap page content in `<div className="page">` (gutter + max-width). Type utilities: `.t-display .t-h1 .t-h2 .t-body .t-small .t-label .t-muted .t-faint .num`.
- **The web view reads left to right; it is not the phone parked in the middle of a monitor.** Every `.page` was `margin-inline: auto`, which centred each screen in whatever space was left beside the rail — at 1440px that is ~200px of nothing between the navigation and where the content starts, and the same again on the right. It is left-aligned from the rail outward now, and `--content-max` grows to 1280px at 1280px, so the extra width buys content further *right* rather than a wider band of empty margin. Note this was only ever a desktop problem: below `--content-max` the auto margins distributed no spare width and did nothing at all.
  - **Centred is now an exception that has to earn itself**, and four things do: the hero amount over a keypad and the keypad's own keys; the hero at the top of a `ConfirmSheet` or a `ReviewList`; `SuccessScreen`, `LockScreen` and the onboarding screens, which are one task on an otherwise empty page with no rail beside them; and a drag handle, which is a handle. Everything else — a settings list, a status page, a wallet address wrapping over three lines, a marketing paragraph — starts at the left.
  - **`margin: X auto Y` is the shorthand that hides from a search for `margin-inline: auto`.** Four blocks on `/entreprise` stayed centred after the sweep because of it, and the screenshot is what caught them.

## Money flows
- **An account holds more than one currency, and a conversion moves money between them.** `Account.pockets` carries what it holds outside its home currency; a pocket appears the first time money lands in it and disappears when it empties, because fifteen zero balances is a filing cabinet rather than a wallet. `api.fx.convert` debits one and credits the other and writes **two ledger lines**, one per currency — a single row could only show one side of the operation. Until this existed, `/convertir` quoted a rate, showed a toast reading « converti », and **moved nothing at all**: no balance changed, no transaction was recorded. An app that tells somebody their money has moved when it has not is doing the one thing a money product may never do, and no screenshot would ever have caught it — `pnpm e2e:flows` does, by reading the balance before and after.

- **The keypad and the summary are never on screen together — in every flow, not just one.** The owner asked for this twice. `StepFlow` (`features/shared`) is the shape, written once: a list of steps, one per screen, with « Étape N sur M », a back arrow that walks *steps* rather than leaving the route, and a sticky action clearing the nav pill. From 1024px it groups into a single form and drops the counter, per the kit's « optimize for devices » rule. A recap beside a live keypad is a total that is still changing, and it pushed itself under the fold anyway.
  - `/envoyer` — méthode → destinataire → montant → aperçu (three steps for an internal transfer, which has no recipient to fill in).
  - `/epargne/deposer` · `/epargne/retirer` · `/crypto/:id/acheter` · `/crypto/:id/vendre` — montant → aperçu.
  - `/convertir` — devises → montant → aperçu. `/crypto/:id/envoyer` — destination → quantité → aperçu.
  - `Ajouter des fonds` still has its own stepping: it was the worked example that all of this came from, and its desktop layout puts a balance card in an aside rather than grouping sections. Converting it is a redesign, not a refactor.
- **The step lives in the URL, because a step can leave the route and come back.** The recipient screen sends you to `/envoyer/operateurs` to pick a rail, which re-mounts the flow — with the step in component state that round trip dumped you on step one having chosen an operator for a form you then had to walk to again. It is written with `replace`, so the phone's back gesture leaves the flow rather than reversing through it; the header's arrow is what walks back a step. The form's state travels to the picker and back in the query string: the picker used to link to a bare `/envoyer?operateur=…`, throwing away the name, the method and the amount.
- **The aperçu and the confirmation sheet are built from one array.** `ReviewList` takes `ConfirmSheet`'s own `SummaryLine[]`, so a flow states what it is about to do twice and cannot state it two ways. This app has been bitten by exactly that: the trade screen's preview promised « ≈ 1.6976 SNTS » for an order the server filled as one share. The two trade flows are the deliberate exception — the sheet's figures come from the server's quote and the aperçu says « prix indicatif » on its face.
- **The keypad does arithmetic** (`operators` on `Keypad`, `calculator` on `AmountEntry`, reducer in `src/lib/calc.ts`). Someone splitting a bill or sending three months of rent should not have to leave for the phone's calculator. **One pending operation, resolved live**: no `=`, and no precedence — `100 + 20 × 3` is 360 here, because precedence on a two-line display is how calculators trick people. Pressing a second operator settles the first, so the hero is always the answer and the line under it shows the working. The result rounds to the currency's minor unit, so a third of 10 000 F CFA is payable in francs. A multiplier is formatted as a **count, not money**: "25 000 F CFA × 3", never "× 3 F CFA". The running state lives inside `AmountEntry` and resyncs against **what it last emitted**, never against its own resolved value — an empty field emits `''` while resolving to `'0'`, and comparing those two loops the render forever.

- **The operators live on their own page** (`/envoyer/operateurs`). "e-Transfer" was one segment of a three-way control, and behind that word sit fifteen rails that work nothing alike — a phone number in Dakar, a tag in London, a counter where cash is collected in Bamako. A picker that long does not belong inside a form someone is already filling in. The families (`mobile_money`, `wallet`, `remittance`, `bank`) are **grouped, not ranked**: which rail is right depends entirely on where the recipient is standing, so the page says what each needs, costs and takes, and refuses to imply an order of merit. Every operator carries an explicit `mark` because initials collide — MTN MoMo and Moov Money both reduce to "MM", and two operators that look identical is the one thing a picker must not do. An operator that is not connected is still listed, muted and not tappable: knowing it is coming is worth something, pretending it works is not. **Fees and limits are demonstration values** and the page says so.
- **An indivisible asset is quoted in whole units, and the preview says the same thing the order does.** A share of Sonatel cannot be split on the BRVM, and the asset declares it (`decimals: 0`). The trade screen used to divide the amount by the price and show « ≈ 1.6976 SNTS » for 50 000 F CFA with a total of 50 000, while the server bought one share for 29 453 — the confirmation sheet promising one thing and the receipt delivering another. Both sides now round to the asset's own precision, **down** on a buy (nobody is charged for more than they asked), and recompute the total from the quantity actually traded. A montant that does not reach one unit is refused with a sentence that says the price.
- **An operator's fee is the user's fee.** Wave takes 1 %, MoneyGram 2.5 % plus a fixed amount. The send sheet showed « Frais 0 F CFA » under a note saying none were charged — true of Keewal Meere's own share, and irrelevant to the person paying. The fee is computed from the chosen operator, shown as « Frais Wave », added to a « Total débité », counted against the balance, and returned by the API as `fee`.
- **The chosen operator travels with the request.** `TransferRequest.providerId` was missing entirely: the picker set it in the URL and the transfer left without it, so nothing downstream could know which rail to use or whose fee applied. And `recipient.email` is `recipient.handle` — the rail decides what identifies someone, and the rule lives in `src/lib/transferHandle.ts` so the form and the back-end cannot disagree about it. They did: the form asked for a phone number and the server refused it as an invalid e-mail.
- **A wire is not an e-Transfer with a different label.** `/envoyer` has three methods, and the `bancaire` branch collects **bank coordinates** — account holder, IBAN, BIC/SWIFT, motif — not a name and an email. `src/lib/iban.ts` does the real ISO 13616 check (first four characters to the end, letters as 10–35, mod 97 must equal 1), taken in seven-digit chunks because a 34-character IBAN becomes a 68-digit number that `Number()` cannot hold exactly. The UEMOA and CEMAC countries all issue IBANs, so a Dakar wire is validated exactly like a Paris one; the length table is per-country because a wrong-length IBAN still passes mod 97 about one time in 97. Each failure gets **its own sentence** — "IBAN invalide" on a number copied off a statement says nothing about where to look. The IBAN is grouped in fours as it is typed, and it is the line under the name on the confirmation sheet, because it is what the receiving bank acts on if the name and the account disagree.

Every buy / sell / transfer / deposit / withdrawal: amount → **Sheet de confirmation** (montant, frais/spread explicites, total) → état de succès explicite → gestion d'erreur (`ApiError.code`: `insufficient_funds`, `validation`, `offline`, `network`). Transactions appear as « En attente » immediately (API events patch the cache), then settle.

## The public page
- **`/entreprise` is the company page, and it sits outside both route guards.** A page that
  redirects a signed-in visitor to their dashboard is a page nobody can link to, so it is
  neither `RequireAuth` nor `RequireAnonymous`. `/bienvenue` links to it; it links back.
- **Every figure on it is one the codebase can point at** — the treaty peg from
  `CFA_PER_EUR`, the tiers from `SPREADS`, the count from `CURRENCY_ORDER.length`. There is
  no "milliards sous gestion", no "clients satisfaits", no approval claimed. The footer says
  plainly that the app holds no money, executes no order and is agréée by no authority, and
  that paragraph is the most important one on the page — a money product that is vague there
  is doing the one thing it must not.
- **The peg is formatted, never typed.** Hand-writing "655,957" prints six hundred thousand
  francs to the euro under the app's own punctuation rule. It goes through `formatNumber`
  like every other number.
- **~~Copy is centred from 768px and left-aligned below it.~~** It reads left to right at
  every width now, like the rest of the app — and the original reasoning always pointed that
  way: centring stops working at about three lines, because each one starts somewhere
  different and the eye has to hunt for it, and these paragraphs run to six.
- **The header is a floating pill, not a bar.** Navigation floats at every width in this app;
  a marketing page that flattens its chrome against the viewport edge stops reading as the
  same product.

## States
Loading → `Skeleton*` (never a spinner for balances/lists). Empty → `EmptyState`. Error → `ErrorState` with Réessayer. Offline → `OfflineBanner` (mounted in the shell) + keep cached data visible.
- **An empty state is a statement of fact, and must never be what loading looks like.** A screen whose query depends on something still arriving — an account id, say — passes `undefined` and the query is *held* rather than run against a placeholder. Passing `'pending'` as an account id returned an empty list, so the transaction section flashed « Aucune transaction sur ce compte » a beat before the rows appeared.
- **A screen that cannot be downloaded must not take the app with it.** Every page is its own chunk, fetched when first opened, and on a patchy mobile connection that fetch fails — which used to throw where nothing caught it: React unmounted everything and the screen went **white**. Tapping a tab in a taxi made the application disappear. `RouteBoundary` sits *inside* `AppShell`, so the navigation, the offline banner and the lock survive and the way out is one tap to a page already loaded. `retryImport` gives the fetch three attempts with a widening gap first, which swallows most of it. And the boundary recovers by itself: a rejected `import()` stays rejected inside `React.lazy` for the life of the document, so the only real retry is a reload — taken half a second after `online` (the event fires a little before a phone's signal is usable), and once more if a reload still comes back empty while the device claims to be online. Two automatic reloads, counted in `sessionStorage`, then it stops and offers the button: a page that reloads itself forever is worse than one that waits to be asked.

## Accessibility
Keyboard-complete; `:focus-visible` rings; ARIA roles on tabs (`SegmentedControl`), dialogs (`Sheet`), switches; long-form `aria-label` on amounts (`Money`, `AmountDisplay`); `prefers-reduced-motion` → 0 ms.

- **A focus ring that exists is not a focus ring that can be seen, and a test pins the difference.** The keyboard walk looks for an outline or a box-shadow, and is right to — `Field` draws its ring on a wrapper on purpose — but a ring the same value as what is behind it passes that check exactly as well as a good one, and the contrast audit cannot cover it either, because it measures text and a ring is a border. `src/styles/tokens.test.ts` computes `--accent` against every **opaque** ground a focusable control can land on and holds WCAG 2.2's 3:1. Measured: light 7.21 on the page, 6.41 on the sheet, 7.34 on a keypad key; dark 13.03, 14.33, 8.43 — plenty of room, which is exactly why it is worth pinning, since the way it breaks is somebody nudging `--accent` for a *text* audit and taking the ring with it. **Two tokens are deliberately excluded and both exclusions are the finding**: `--card-surface` is the virtual payment card, always dark in both themes, and `VirtualCard.tsx` renders nothing focusable — its pair measures 2.34:1 and asserting on it would fail the build over a combination that cannot occur (it is also *not* the `Card` component's fill, which is the `--surface` family; the name misleads). And `--fill-subtle` is `oklch(… / 0.08)`, a translucent wash rather than a ground — what sits behind a chip is that wash *over* something already in the list, and comparing the ring to the undiluted ink gave 2.29:1 and 1.21:1, two numbers for a colour that never reaches a screen. A second assertion now refuses any ground carrying an alpha, so that mistake cannot be made again silently.
- **« Keyboard-complete » is measured, by `pnpm e2e:keyboard`** — every screen at both
  widths, driven with Tab, Shift-Tab, arrows, Enter and Escape and nothing else. It fails on a stop
  that takes focus without showing it, a stop that is invisible or `aria-hidden`, a page Tab
  cannot leave, a stop reachable in one direction only, a composite that costs more than one
  Tab or whose arrows move nothing, a skip link that does not skip, and a `Sheet` that lets
  focus out, ignores Escape, or drops focus instead of returning it to the opener. **And that an
  amount can be *typed*** — the walk proves every keypad key is reachable and ringed, which is
  exactly why it never noticed that the number row did nothing at all. **The ring
  may live on a wrapper**: `Field` draws it on its container via `:focus-within` so the label,
  the leading icon and the input read as one control, and the check looks three levels up
  rather than forcing the ring onto the input.
