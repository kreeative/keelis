# Keewal Meere — conventions for contributors (human or agent)

Keewal Meere is a mobile-first fintech web app for **Africa** (React 19 + TypeScript + Vite). Products: Marchés (African equities + crypto), Chèque (spending + card), Épargne, and Change. UI copy is **French (fr-SN)**, with **en-NG** as the second locale; number/date formatting is localised via `src/lib/format.ts`.

## Money is multi-currency, and the currency decides its own punctuation
- **`src/lib/currency.ts` is the registry** — sixteen currencies: the two CFA francs, the major African units, plus EUR and USD as anchors. `Currency` is that union; `src/api/types.ts` re-exports it. It was `'CAD'`, a one-member union, and every African ambition in the roadmap was blocked behind it.
- **Not every currency has cents.** XOF, XAF, UGX and RWF are quoted in whole units; TND is quoted in *three* decimals (millimes). `formatMoney` asks the currency rather than forcing two, and the registry's `decimals` is pinned to `Intl`'s own answer by a test, so arithmetic and display cannot drift apart. Rounding happens **once, at the end** of a conversion (`roundTo`) — rounding to whole francs mid-calculation compounds across a two-leg cross.
- **Digits carry one punctuation app-wide: comma groups, point decimates** — `1,234,567.89`, in French as in English. This deliberately overrides French typography (thin-space groups, comma decimal) on the owner's instruction: it is what African fintech apps and every price feed already use, so a figure reads the same on screen as on the statement it is compared against. It lives in `joinParts`, so `formatMoney`, `formatNumber` and `formatPercent` cannot drift apart, and the keypad's decimal key shows a point. The *words* stay French. **The keypad's internal string still uses a comma** — `keypadReduce` and its tests work on that, and only the display converts — so do not "fix" it to match.
- **The locale matters for more than commas.** `fr-CA` renders XOF as "XOF"; `fr-SN` renders it "F CFA", which is what it is called where it is spent. That one substitution is most of what makes the app read as African rather than Canadian.
- **A symbol is never inferred from the currency code.** `splitMoney` pulls it out of `Intl.formatToParts` — "F CFA", "₦", "GH₵", "€" — and reports which side the locale puts it on. Code that compared the code to `'CAD'` and assumed `'$'` is exactly what broke when the app moved.
- **The CFA peg is a treaty, not a quote.** XOF and XAF are fixed to the euro at **exactly 655.957**, so `lib/fx.ts` treats that pair as a constant: the rate does not move and the spread is the only variable. They are 1:1 with each other and still charged, because two central banks settle them.
- **The spread is stated, never buried in a worse rate.** `quote()` returns the mid rate, the applied rate, the tier and the fee *as money in the currency being sold*; `/convertir` shows all of them before the user commits. Tiers price legs, not sentiment: anchor 0.5 %, one African leg 1.2 %, African cross 1.8 %, pegged 1.0 %.
- **Quote a pair in the direction that reads.** XOF→EUR is 0,0015244 before the spread and 0,0015061 after — rounded for display both read "0,0015" and the margin looks like zero. Inverted (655,96 against 663,92 francs to the euro) the same spread is plainly visible. That is how a bureau de change writes it on the board.
- **The rates in `lib/fx.ts` are demonstration rates**, anchored to real orders of magnitude but not a feed. Every surface that shows one says so. The two pegged numbers are the exception and are exact.

## Brand
The marks live in `src/components/Wordmark.tsx`.

**The product was renamed from Keelis to Keewal Meere, and the wordmark artwork did not
survive it.** The old wordmark was a bezier path traced from the brand sheet — and that
path spelled *Keelis*. Artwork cannot be re-traced for a name it was never drawn for, so
the name is **typeset in Poppins as a stopgap** until new artwork exists. It is
deliberately not set in the brand face: EtherealDemo-ExtraBold has no commercial licence,
and the old wordmark only escaped that by shipping as outlines rather than as type.
**The monogram survives**, because the new name also starts with K.

- **The monogram is an image on purpose.** The sheet draws the K disc *embossed* — a raised
  letter, a lit rim, a soft shadow — and tracing it to a flat vector throws all of that
  away. `public/brand/mark-ink.png` and `mark-paper.png` are the sheet's own two versions;
  `--mark-disc` picks one by theme, so only the used one is fetched, and `tone="ink"` /
  `tone="paper"` pins one where the surface decides instead (the virtual card is always
  dark, so it always takes the paper disc). Do not replace it with a drawn circle.
- **The mark gets clear space, not paragraph spacing.** A logo set with the same air as a
  line of body text reads as crowded. Any screen that pairs the wordmark with a heading
  gives it `.mark-clear` — at least its own height before the next thing starts. Check the
  container's gap before reusing the class: it adds to it rather than replacing it.
- **The brand face is EtherealDemo-ExtraBold, a *demo* release not licensed for commercial
  use.** The outlines ship as artwork; never set the name as live text in it, and never add
  it as a webfont. Licensing it is the owner's decision.

## Non-negotiable design rules (enforced by `scripts/check-design.mjs` and `e2e/screenshots.mjs`)
- **All visual values come from `src/styles/tokens.css`.** Never write a colour literal, px font-size, shadow, blur or gradient in a component. Use `var(--…)`.
- **The rim is what makes glass read as glass, and it is *measured*.** Sampling the owner's reference on all four edges of a floating nav pill, the boundary hairline reads ~100 against a ground of 44 and a glass interior of 51 — white at roughly **0.24**, the brightest thing in the control. The dark theme carried 0.07 and the edge simply vanished; anything that floats, including the mobile pill and the active capsule inside it, carries the rim, or it reads as a smudge rather than a piece of material. `--glass-rim` is three insets on `.glass::after`: a hard top edge where light lands, a hairline tracing the whole boundary (the material's thickness), and light pooling along the bottom inner edge — the lensing you see at the edge of anything transparent. It rides on a pseudo-element *on purpose*: a second `box-shadow` on the element itself would be replaced by the hover, active and focus states rather than added to them.
- **Material.** Surfaces are glass: `.glass` (or `.glass-strong`) + an `.elev-*` class, or the `Card` component, which composes both. Glass = translucent background + `var(--glass-blur)` + a hairline `--glass-border` top highlight. There is an opaque `@supports` fallback; never assume backdrop-filter exists.
- **Elevation is layered, never a single blur.** Use `--elev-1/2/3` (ambient contact + direct drop + deep lift) and `--elev-2-hover`. A hand-rolled `box-shadow` fails the design check.
- **Tactile physics.** Interactive surfaces lift with `var(--lift)` (`translateY(-2px) scale(1.01)`) and settle with `var(--press)`; both collapse to `none` under reduced motion. Animate transform and box-shadow only.
- **Ambient ground.** `<AmbientGround />` (mounted once in `App`) paints the field the glass refracts: four overlapping radial fields, so the middle of a tall viewport is not flat. `body` is transparent on purpose — do not give it a background. The ground has to stay *deep* — if the fields sit within a few hundredths of `--surface` in lightness, a translucent pane over them is indistinguishable from an opaque one and the material disappears. Panes are ~50% opaque; the audit measures composited contrast, so push depth until it complains, not further.
- **The palette is monochrome, with exactly three exceptions.** Every colour is a neutral derived from black and white — `oklch(L 0 0)`. A token with chroma > 0, or an `rgb()` whose channels differ, fails the design check. No hue anywhere: not in charts, badges, status, gains or losses. **The first is direction**: `--pos` is green and `--neg` is red, because up and down is the one meaning people read by colour before they read anything. Both are measured — 5.49:1 and 5.87:1 on the page — and colour is never the *only* carrier: the explicit sign, the glyph and the weight still say it, so every state survives a greyscale screenshot. **The second is the asset marks** in `src/components/AssetIcon.tsx`, which keep each token's real colours — a logo is not decoration, it is how someone finds Bitcoin in a list without reading. The design guard exempts that file *by name*; putting colour anywhere else fails the check, and that is deliberate.
- **The third exception is the aurora wash** (`--aurora-1…5`, `features/home/AuroraGround.tsx`), and it is the narrowest: five very pale fields drifting under the **home sheet only**, in the **light theme only**. It carries no meaning, never sits under text on its own ground or under a control, and a greyscale screenshot loses nothing by it — which is why it can hold hue where nothing else may. It fades with scroll (full at the top, a 0.12 floor once you are down in the transaction list: the top is where someone lingers, the list is where they want a quiet page). Transform-only drift at 47–73s, so it costs a composite and no more; `prefers-reduced-motion` stops the movement and keeps the colour, because the colour was never the risk. The design guard sanctions it **by name** in both its gradient allowlist and its chroma check — putting a hue anywhere else still fails, and that is the point.
- **Direction is carried by hue *and* by sign.** Gains are `--pos`, losses `--neg` — but the explicit sign (`+` / `−`), the directional glyph and the weight carry it too, so nothing depends on colour alone. Errors resolve to `--neg` and carry their glyph — the glyph is what survives a greyscale screenshot, the colour is what makes a rejected field findable on a full form. Warnings stay `--ink-900` with their glyph. Keep `signed` on amounts that can go either way.
- **`--cta` is a fill only** (ink in light, paper in dark), reserved for primary buttons; text on it is `--on-cta`. Links, ghost buttons and focus rings use `--accent`; accent text on `--accent-soft` uses `--accent-text`.
- `--ink-300` is **not** for text (fails contrast); use `--ink-400` for labels/tertiary text.
- **The chart line is smoothed with *monotone* interpolation** (`smoothPath`), never a plain spline. A Catmull-Rom or cardinal spline curves prettily and overshoots: between two points it swings past both and draws a peak higher than the highest price in the data. On a price chart that is a lie. Monotone (Fritsch–Carlson) clamps the tangents so the curve never leaves the interval its endpoints define — the corners round off, flat runs stay flat, and every high and low on screen actually happened. Covered by tests; do not swap it for something smoother.
- **The chart's area is texture that fades with depth.** A tinted fill under the curve has nothing to tint in a monochrome palette, so `Chart` fills the area with a lattice of 1px dots — it reads as filled without needing colour, and it is an SVG `<pattern>`, so it does not break the no-gradients rule. The lattice is masked by a vertical fade that reaches zero at 80% of the plot height, so the texture reads as something the line casts rather than a slab under it. The fade runs down the **plot box**, not down from the line — measured on the reference, ink at a given height is the same whether the line is 17px or 527px above it. It draws a dot on the last point, and on scrub a cursor line and a **ring** on the point being read — a ring, because a filled dot covers the very shape the cursor is asking about. **There is no tooltip.** The screen's own hero is the readout: `onHover` swaps the balance for the scrubbed value and the delta line for its timestamp, and a floating box repeating that sat directly on top of the number it was duplicating. One readout, where the eye already is; the scrubbed value still reaches screen readers through a visually-hidden `role="status"`. While scrubbing, the series is drawn twice — dimmed for the whole period, then again clipped to everything left of the cursor — so what you have scrubbed past stays lit and what you have not reached recedes. **There is no dashed baseline** — the opening-value line was removed on the owner's instruction: it ran horizontally through the middle of the plot and read as chart junk rather than as a reference. Do not reinstate it. The viewBox tracks the **measured** width via a callback ref — not a fixed 1000 — so the drawing space equals the painting space and dots stay round. A plain `useEffect` cannot do this: the chart renders a skeleton first, so there is no `<svg>` to observe yet.
- **The pro chart is `CandleChart`, and it is offered from 1024px only.** A candle packs four numbers into one mark; below a laptop there is neither the width to separate the bodies nor a pointer precise enough to read them, so the phone keeps the line — which is the right chart there. It is a **toggle, defaulted off**: the line is what most people want, and a candle chart shown to someone who did not ask for one is worse than no chart. Its candles are **bucketed from the very series the line draws**, never a second dataset — a pro view that disagreed with the figure above it would be worse than none. `candleCount()` sizes the buckets at ≥5 points each: slice a 96-point day into 48 candles and every bucket holds two points, so high === open, low === close, and every wick disappears (covered by a test — that regression is invisible in code and obvious on screen). Direction is carried by **fill as well as hue** — filled when it closed up, hollow when it closed down — so it survives a greyscale screenshot like every other state. **There is deliberately no volume histogram**: volume cannot be derived from a price series, and inventing one for an app aimed at real investors would look like information while being none. It needs a market data feed.
- **The destination is called « Actifs », not « Crypto ».** It lists African equities *and* crypto, so it cannot be named after one of them. The route stays `/crypto` — renaming a path breaks links and buys nothing.
- **A market list is scanned by ticker.** `AssetIcon` + the symbol as the row's title, the full name in the subtitle, and `Delta` carrying `amount` so the move shows in dollars *and* percent — a percentage alone does not say whether it was worth 4 $ or 400 $.
- **More filters than fit on a phone → `ChipBar`, not `SegmentedControl`.** The segmented control divides a fixed width between its segments, so it caps out at three or four; the chip bar is content-sized and scrolls past the gutter, and that overflowing chip is the affordance saying there is more.
- Assets are drawn with `AssetIcon`, never a monogram `Avatar`, wherever the symbol is known. `AvatarStack` takes `assets` for the same reason.
- No emoji. Gradients only in the token/base/AmbientGround layer. `backdrop-filter` only via `--glass-blur`.
- Text ≥ 12px (`--fs-label`). Tap targets ≥ 44px (`--tap`).
- Motion 150–250 ms via `--dur-*` and `--ease`; nothing bounces. Reduced motion is handled globally.
- Every screen has one dominant number (`AmountDisplay`) or one dominant title (`PageHeader` / `.t-h1`).
- **The typeface is Poppins** (`--font-sans`), self-hosted under SIL OFL in `src/styles/fonts.css` and `public/fonts/`. Never add a third-party font request — the offline and single-file builds depend on it. Poppins ships as static weights, so only the four the token layer names are declared (400 / 500 / 600 / 700); adding a weight means adding its two faces. Financial figures use the same family with `tabular-nums` so columns still align.
- **700 is for text inside a control, not for a heading.** Buttons, chips and the uppercase `.t-section` carry `--fw-bold`; a heading in running text sits at `--fw-h1` / `--fw-h2` (600) with negative tracking. A page title that reached for `--fw-bold` was borrowing the button's weight.
- **Headings track negative too.** The kit's Foundations page lists positive tracking on its headings; at our sizes in Poppins that pulls a sentence apart — `--ls-h1` is −0.02em and `--ls-h2` −0.015em. Only the uppercase micro-label keeps positive tracking, where it belongs.
- **~~Headings track positive, running text tracks negative.~~** Measured from the kit's named styles: H1 22/29/+1px, H3 16/21/+0.5, H4 14/19/+0.5, H6 11/15/+1.5 uppercase, body/large 16/26/−0.25, body/med 14/20/0. Headings are weight 700.
- **The currency symbol is part of the figure, not an annotation.** Same size, same weight, same ink as the digits (`.symbol` in `AmountDisplay`). What recedes is a *unit* — « BTC », « USD » — which is a word, not a symbol (`.unit`). The narrow no-break space French sets before the symbol is `0.18em`, in em so it holds at every size.
- **`--ls-numeric` is negative** (−0.02em). At display size the counters carry themselves and positive tracking only pulls the number apart.
- **Money is weight 500** (`--fw-numeric`) with `--ls-numeric` at −0.035em — medium and tight, not bold. 900 turns a balance into a slab and 700 is still shouting; at 48px the size already carries the hierarchy and the counters carry themselves, so the weight comes down and the tracking comes in. Poppins ships 400 / 500 / 600 / 700 only — anything between falls back to the next face up, silently. It applies through `Money`, `AmountDisplay`, `.t-display`, `.figures`, `AmountEntry` and `ListRow` values; change the token, never a component.
- **`Button` takes `to` and then renders a real `<a>`**, the way `Card` already does. A
  destination has to survive a middle-click, a long-press and a "copy link address", which an
  `onClick` on a `<button>` silently does not. Keep `onClick` for actions.
- Give short controls an explicit `min-width: var(--tap)` so they never fall under 44px.
- The spacing scale is the kit's: 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 (`--sp-1`…`--sp-8`, plus 96 for documentation). The screen's side margin is `--gutter` — 16px on mobile, the kit's own value.
- Radii follow the kit's scale (2 / 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32): `--r-card` 16px for a card, `--r-panel` 24px for a container that holds cards, `--r-field` 16px for inner elements, `--r-sheet` 32px, `--r-pill` for pills and buttons.
- **Three text roles, three classes, no improvising.** `.t-section` is a section's title (14/18, 700, uppercase, `--ls-section` = −0.002em, full ink — all four from the kit's own title component; uppercase at 14px tracks essentially not at all, and the Foundations H6's +0.14em belongs to an 11px style, not this one) — `SectionHeader` and any bare heading a screen writes both use it, so they cannot drift apart. `.t-name` names a value below it (« Solde total », « Chèque », « Quantité ») — 14px, sentence case, `--ink-600`: it labels the figure, it does not shout over it. `.t-label` (12px, uppercase, tracked +0.135em) is for true micro-annotations only — form labels, column heads. A page title is never `.t-label`.
- **Every primary destination opens with `AppBar`** below 768px: bell (carrying the unread dot) on the left, the screen's name centred, profile on the right. It is the only way to reach `/notifications` on a phone. Sub-pages keep `PageHeader`; from 768px the rail carries both and the bar hides itself.
- **Keypad keys are raised lozenges.** Every screen where someone types a number — an amount, a NIP — uses `Keypad`, and its keys are physical: `--r-pill`, a `--key-face` fill, the hairline rim, `--elev-1`, lifting on hover and *sinking* on press to `--key-press` with the shadow removed. They are solid, not glass: twelve blurred panes is the most expensive way to draw a keypad and buys nothing a tappable key needs. **`--key-face` / `--key-press` exist because no existing token survives both themes here** — "raised" means nearer the light, and `--surface` is the top layer in light but the bottom one in dark, while `--fill-subtle` is an ink wash in light and a *paper* wash in dark, which would make a pressed key glow instead of sink. Reach for the pair rather than picking a surface by hand.

- **`Switch` carries a checkmark when on.** In a palette with no hue, a fill-only toggle reads as "dark grey vs darker grey"; the knob's shape and the check make the state legible. Anatomy is the kit's: a 56×30 track at 16px radius, a dark knob on a pale bordered track when off, a pale knob with a check on a filled track when on, both lifted by `--elev-item`.
- **Section rhythm comes from the spacing-application tokens, not from `--sp-*` picked by hand.** The kit's second spacing board says where each step goes: `--space-elements` (8) inside a component, `--space-items` (16) between list items, `--space-block` (16 → 24) from a section's header to its content, `--space-section` (40 → 48) between sections. Only those last two grow, at the kit's own 1024px breakpoint. Reach for `--sp-*` for local spacing; never for the gap between two sections.
- **A stepped task becomes one form at 1024px** (`useLargeScreen`, the kit's « optimize for devices »): on a large screen a keyboard and a mouse beat a wizard, so group the steps and drop the step counter — but keep the mobile flow exactly as it was, since that is the one people know from the app. `Ajouter des fonds` is the worked example.
- **Warn before, not after** (`Callout`, the kit's « reinforce sense of security »): a consequence worth knowing goes in the flow beside the control, not only in the confirmation sheet. `note` is the inline line, `panel` the boxed form.
- **A field's message carries a glyph.** With no hue, a success line, a warning and an error are the same grey 14px text; `Field`/`SelectField`/`TextAreaField` take `success` and `warning` alongside `error`, and all three render with their icon. A `hint` stays bare — it is not a state. `warning` is a caution that does not block: the message appears, the field's border stays neutral. `AmountEntry`'s error line carries the same alert glyph.
- **Choosing among options is a `ChoiceList`**, not a `<select>`: the kit builds its list selector out of universal rows with a radio trailing, so each option can carry the facts that decide the choice (fee, delay, last payment). It is a real radiogroup — one tab stop, arrows move and select, disabled options are skipped — and takes a `footer` for the escape hatch. Keep `SelectField` for flat lists of plain labels.
- **`ListRow stack`** is the kit's mobile row: below 768px the value drops under the text instead of competing with it for width, while the leading mark and the trailing control span both lines. Use it when a row carries both a long label and a value.
- **`StatGrid`** is the kit's Stats block, with both of its shapes: label-left / value-right rows under 768px, a label-above-value grid beyond it.
- **Unread markers** use `StatusDot`: 8px with a 2px ring of `--surface`, and `corner` anchors it to the top-right of the *glyph's* wrapper, never the 44px control around it.
- **Icons** are a 20px box (`--icon`, the reference kit's grid size) holding a ~16px glyph, lucide outlines at 1.5px stroke. `Icon` defaults to 20; pass `size` only where a screen needs otherwise. The `ICONS` map ends with a semantic group taken from the kit — `deposit`, `withdraw`, `transfer`, `recurring`, `cheque`, `wire`, `crypto`, `invest`, `split`, `face-id`, `warning` and friends. Prefer the semantic name over a generic arrow: it says what the action means and survives a change of glyph.

## Architecture
- `src/api/types.ts` — the `KeewalApi` contract. `src/api/mock/` implements it (latency, events, price ticks, optimistic settlement). Screens import `api` from `@/api` only.
- `src/store/` — `useQuery` (cache; data never drops to undefined during refetch), `useMutation`, `QK` query keys, `useSession` (auth + PIN lock), `useSettings` (theme / locale / hidden balances), `useToast`, `useMarket` / `useAsset` (live prices), `useOnline`.
- `src/components/` — the component library. Import from `@/components`. Do not create parallel primitives; extend these.
- `src/features/<area>/` — screens. Routes live in `src/shell/routes.tsx` (French paths).
- **Navigation floats at every width.** The desktop rail is the mobile pill stood on its end — detached from all four edges, glass, `--r-pill`, `--elev-2` and the rim. Flattening it against the viewport edge is what makes the whole screen stop reading as glass. **Mobile navigation is a floating pill**, not a bar welded to the edge: centred, glass, `--elev-2`, with the active destination in a filled capsule and icons only (each link carries its name as an `aria-label`). Anything sticky at the bottom of a screen must clear it — `calc(var(--navbar-height) + var(--navbar-gap) * 2 + var(--safe-bottom) + …)`.
- **Accueil is a canvas with a sheet riding over it, and the canvas follows the theme.** It used to be dark in both themes, the way the virtual card still is; the owner asked for it white on the light theme. The `--canvas-*` set therefore has two versions — in light they resolve to the page's own values, so the token island inside `.canvas` becomes a no-op and nothing has to be restyled; in dark they carry the dark island exactly as before. It is **not** `--card-surface`: the virtual card is still always dark. `index.html`'s `theme-color` is the canvas's own measured colour (#fcfcfc / #0f0f0f), because the top of the page is the canvas and a `theme-color` taken from the page background is what put a pale seam above the app. The canvas holds the balance, the curve, and — at its foot, under the curve — the two actions people actually take: the balance and its shape are what the screen is for, and the actions are what you do after reading them. The canvas carries its own colour up past the viewport (`.canvas::before`) so an iOS rubber-band overscroll reveals more black instead of the light ambient ground. **The sheet is `--sheet-ground`, not `--surface`** — grey, a step below the white glass cards riding on it. When the sheet was `--surface` too, the cards had nothing but their own shadow to be seen by; the separation is the material, not the fill, and the token is a step below the card in *both* themes rather than flipping. The canvas the sheet carries everything else and rides up over the canvas's rounded bottom. It is always dark, in both themes, the way the payment card is — and it does not restyle its children: it **redefines the tokens they read** (`--ink-900`, `--surface`, `--pos`, `--glass-bg`…) from the `--canvas-*` set, so a chart, a segmented control and a button behave inside it exactly as they would on a dark theme. Adding a component there costs nothing; adding a hand-written dark style costs the next person an hour.
- **A hero figure never wraps — it shrinks to fit.** "10,792,539 XOF" broke across two lines mid-number and mid-symbol, which is the one thing a hero amount cannot do. `AmountDisplay` counts the characters and passes a ceiling in container units (`--amount-fit`): a tabular Poppins digit is ~0.55em wide, so N characters fit at `(100 / (N × 0.55)) cqi`, and the CSS takes `min()` of that and the display size. Short amounts are therefore untouched at full size, long ones step down exactly as far as they need to, and it adapts to the *container* rather than the viewport — no measuring loop, no layout thrash. Measured: 21,838,776 F CFA renders on one line at 40.7px on a 390px screen and 32.7px on a 320px one.
- **A figure is one size throughout — and it carries no `,00`.** `formatMoney` drops the cents on a whole number by default (`alwaysCents` opts back in), because `,00` carries no information; a dozen call sites were already opting out of it one at a time before it became the default. The decision is made on the value *rendered*, not the one passed in, or a tiny negative clamped to zero would print `0,00 $` while a true zero printed `0 $`. What remains — real cents, and the currency symbol — is set at the same size, weight and ink as the dollars. `AmountDisplay` used to halve the cents and grey them; the owner asked for one size and there is no `.cents` split any more.
- **One curve per screen, and it belongs to that screen.** Accueil's chart is the *whole* wealth; the Crypto page draws its own book. The account cards and the `Actifs` row carry **no sparkline** — direction there is a signed figure in `--pos` / `--neg`, money *and* percent, because a percentage alone does not say whether the move was worth 4 000 F CFA or 400 000. A second small curve on a tile competes with the one above it and says less.
- **The book's curve is computed, not generated** (`api.crypto.portfolioHistory`): each holding's real price series times the quantity actually held, summed point for point. A portfolio chart drawn from its own random walk would disagree with the assets listed under it, and the first person to notice would be right.
- **`Actifs` sits between Épargne and Crypto** on Accueil, not after it — the holdings are what this app is principally about, so they come before the wallet that happens to hold some of them. `AccountCards` takes `beforeCrypto` for the slot.
- **Every section sits on a surface.** Balance, chart and period control are one statement, so they share one `Card`; a list goes in a card; interest figures go in a card. Content floating loose on the page ground is the exception, not the default — the page ground is what the glass refracts, not a place to set text. Section headers stay *outside* the card they introduce.
- Layout: wrap page content in `<div className="page">` (gutter + max-width). Type utilities: `.t-display .t-h1 .t-h2 .t-body .t-small .t-label .t-muted .t-faint .num`.

## Money flows
- **The keypad and the summary are never on screen together.** On a phone, `Ajouter des fonds` is three screens — provenance → montant → aperçu → (confirmation). A recap beside a live keypad is a total that is still changing, and it pushed itself under the fold anyway. `grouped` (≥1024px) still shows the whole task as one form, per the kit's « optimize for devices » rule. The amount **leads** the summary: splitting the keypad off took away the only place it was visible.
- **The keypad does arithmetic** (`operators` on `Keypad`, `calculator` on `AmountEntry`, reducer in `src/lib/calc.ts`). Someone splitting a bill or sending three months of rent should not have to leave for the phone's calculator. **One pending operation, resolved live**: no `=`, and no precedence — `100 + 20 × 3` is 360 here, because precedence on a two-line display is how calculators trick people. Pressing a second operator settles the first, so the hero is always the answer and the line under it shows the working. The result rounds to the currency's minor unit, so a third of 10 000 F CFA is payable in francs. A multiplier is formatted as a **count, not money**: "25 000 F CFA × 3", never "× 3 F CFA". The running state lives inside `AmountEntry` and resyncs against **what it last emitted**, never against its own resolved value — an empty field emits `''` while resolving to `'0'`, and comparing those two loops the render forever.

- **The operators live on their own page** (`/envoyer/operateurs`). "e-Transfer" was one segment of a three-way control, and behind that word sit fifteen rails that work nothing alike — a phone number in Dakar, a tag in London, a counter where cash is collected in Bamako. A picker that long does not belong inside a form someone is already filling in. The families (`mobile_money`, `wallet`, `remittance`, `bank`) are **grouped, not ranked**: which rail is right depends entirely on where the recipient is standing, so the page says what each needs, costs and takes, and refuses to imply an order of merit. Every operator carries an explicit `mark` because initials collide — MTN MoMo and Moov Money both reduce to "MM", and two operators that look identical is the one thing a picker must not do. An operator that is not connected is still listed, muted and not tappable: knowing it is coming is worth something, pretending it works is not. **Fees and limits are demonstration values** and the page says so.
- **A wire is not an e-Transfer with a different label.** `/envoyer` has three methods, and the `bancaire` branch collects **bank coordinates** — account holder, IBAN, BIC/SWIFT, motif — not a name and an email. `src/lib/iban.ts` does the real ISO 13616 check (first four characters to the end, letters as 10–35, mod 97 must equal 1), taken in seven-digit chunks because a 34-character IBAN becomes a 68-digit number that `Number()` cannot hold exactly. The UEMOA and CEMAC countries all issue IBANs, so a Dakar wire is validated exactly like a Paris one; the length table is per-country because a wrong-length IBAN still passes mod 97 about one time in 97. Each failure gets **its own sentence** — "IBAN invalide" on a number copied off a statement says nothing about where to look. The IBAN is grouped in fours as it is typed, and it is the line under the name on the confirmation sheet, because it is what the receiving bank acts on if the name and the account disagree.

Every buy / sell / transfer / deposit / withdrawal: amount → **Sheet de confirmation** (montant, frais/spread explicites, total) → état de succès explicite → gestion d'erreur (`ApiError.code`: `insufficient_funds`, `validation`, `offline`, `network`). Transactions appear as « En attente » immediately (API events patch the cache), then settle.

## The public page
- **`/entreprise` is the company page, and it sits outside both route guards.** A page that
  redirects a signed-in visitor to their dashboard is a page nobody can link to, so it is
  neither `RequireAuth` nor `RequireAnonymous`. `/bienvenue` links to it; it links back.
- **Every figure on it is one the codebase can point at** — the treaty peg from
  `CFA_PER_EUR`, the tiers from `SPREADS`, the count from `CURRENCY_ORDER.length`. There is
  no "milliards sous gestion", no "clients satisfaits", no approval claimed. The footer says
  plainly that the app holds no money, executes no order and is agréée by no authority, and
  that paragraph is the most important one on the page — a money product that is vague there
  is doing the one thing it must not.
- **The peg is formatted, never typed.** Hand-writing "655,957" prints six hundred thousand
  francs to the euro under the app's own punctuation rule. It goes through `formatNumber`
  like every other number.
- **Copy is centred from 768px and left-aligned below it.** Centring stops working at about
  three lines — each one starts somewhere different and the eye has to hunt — and on a phone
  these paragraphs run to six or seven.
- **The header is a floating pill, not a bar.** Navigation floats at every width in this app;
  a marketing page that flattens its chrome against the viewport edge stops reading as the
  same product.
